From daily users to QPS: averages, peaks and read/write ratios

Almost every estimate starts from users. DAU (daily active users) counts distinct users active on a given day; MAU counts them over a month. The DAU/MAU ratio measures how habitual a product is; messaging and social apps run high, utility apps lower. QPS (queries per second) and RPS (requests per second) are used interchangeably for request rate.

The conversion you'll use most

86,400Seconds per day, round to 10^5
~2.6MSeconds per 30-day month
~31.5MSeconds per year, about π × 10^7
~12/sAverage rate of 1 million events per day
Events per dayExact average rateQuick rate, dividing by 10^5
1 million~11.6/s~10/s
10 million~116/s~100/s
100 million~1,160/s~1,000/s
1 billion~11,600/s~10,000/s
10 billion~116,000/s~100,000/s

Dividing by 10^5 instead of 86,400 understates the rate by about 14%. That is well inside estimation error, and the peak factor you multiply by next matters far more. If you want to be safe, say “about 12,000 per second” for a billion a day.

Worked example: a social app

text
Assumptions
  100M DAU
  each user makes 50 read requests/day (feed, profiles, comments)
  each user makes 5 write requests/day (posts, likes, comments)

Daily volume
  reads:  100M x 50 = 5 x 10^9 = 5B/day
  writes: 100M x 5  = 5 x 10^8 = 500M/day

Average rate (divide by 10^5)
  reads:  5 x 10^9 / 10^5 = 5 x 10^4 = 50,000/s
  writes: 5 x 10^8 / 10^5 = 5 x 10^3 =  5,000/s

Peak (factor 3 for a global app)
  reads:  150,000/s
  writes:  15,000/s
  read:write ratio = 10:1

Peak traffic

Systems must be sized for the peak, not the average. Traffic follows a daily cycle, and the more concentrated your users are in time zones and habits, the higher the peak relative to the average. When you know when traffic clusters, compute the peak directly: if 30% of 1 billion daily requests happen in the busiest 3 hours, the peak is 0.3 × 10^9 ÷ 10,800 s ≈ 28,000/s, about 2.4x the 11,600/s average.

Traffic shapeRough peak ÷ averageExample
Global consumer app, users in many time zones~2–3xSocial feed, web search
Single-country or single-city app~4–10xFood delivery dinner rush, regional news
Business app~3–5x in weekday working hours, near zero at nightOffice suite, CRM, internal tools
Event-driven burst~10–100x for minutesFlash sale, concert ticket release, live TV moment
In production
Twitter's 25x burst
In August 2013 Twitter reported a record of 143,199 tweets per second during a TV broadcast of the film Castle in the Sky in Japan, when its steady-state average was about 5,700 tweets per second. Systems that only handle 3x the average fall over on nights like that.

Read/write ratios

Splitting traffic into reads and writes tells you which half of the architecture deserves attention. The ratio is one of the most decision-relevant numbers in an interview.

SystemRough read:writeWhat it implies
Social feed or timeline100:1 or moreCaches, precomputed feeds, read replicas
URL shortener100:1 or moreCache redirects; very cache- and CDN-friendly
Product catalog1,000:1 or moreCDN and cache; writes are rare admin edits
Chat messaging~1:1 to a few:1Write-optimized storage and efficient delivery
Payments and orders~1:1 to 10:1Transactions and strong consistency first
Logs, metrics, IoT telemetryWrite-dominated; most data is never readAppend-only storage, batching, compression
Pitfall
Watch for multipliers
Counting user actions is not the same as counting backend operations. If home feeds are precomputed, one post from a user with 200 followers becomes 200 feed inserts: 5,000 posts/s × 200 = 1 million inserts/s. One page view that calls 8 microservices is 8 internal requests. Find the fan-out factor before you size anything (Chapter 16 covers feed fan-out).
