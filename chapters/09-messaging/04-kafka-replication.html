Kafka replication, ISR and acks

Each partition is replicated to replication factor brokers (3 is the common production choice). One replica is the leader; producers write to it and, by default, consumers read from it. The others are followers that continuously fetch from the leader, exactly like consumers. With rack-aware placement the three replicas live in three availability zones, so losing a zone loses at most one copy.

In-sync replicas and the high watermark

The in-sync replica set (ISR) is the leader plus every follower that has caught up with the leader's log recently, within replica.lag.time.max.ms (30 seconds by default). A follower that falls behind is removed from the ISR and rejoins once caught up. The high watermark is the offset just past the last record that every ISR member has stored. Records below it are committed: consumers can only read up to the high watermark, so they never see a record that could disappear in a leader failover.

Producer acks

SettingLeader replies whenLatencyWhat can be lost
acks=0Immediately; the producer does not waitLowestAnything: a dropped request or broker crash loses data silently
acks=1The leader has appended to its own logLowAcknowledged records if the leader dies before followers copy them
acks=all (default since Kafka 3.0)Every current ISR member has the recordsHighest, one extra replication round tripNothing while at least one ISR member survives, subject to min.insync.replicas

acks=all has a trap: 'all' means all current ISR members. If both followers have dropped out, the ISR is just the leader, and acks=all degrades to acks=1. min.insync.replicas closes the gap. With replication factor 3 and min.insync.replicas=2, a write with acks=all succeeds only if at least two replicas have it; if the ISR shrinks to one, the leader rejects acks=all writes with a NotEnoughReplicas error. That is a deliberate CP choice (Chapter 8): the partition becomes unavailable for writes rather than accepting writes that a single failure could destroy. Reads of already-committed data continue.

Leader failure

  1. Detect
    The controller (a Raft-based KRaft quorum in modern Kafka; ZooKeeper in older versions) notices the broker has stopped heartbeating.
  2. Elect
    For each partition the dead broker led, the controller picks a new leader from the ISR, so every committed record is preserved.
  3. Fence
    The leader epoch is incremented. Requests carrying the old epoch are rejected, so a zombie ex-leader cannot get new writes committed.
  4. Truncate
    When the old leader returns as a follower, it truncates any uncommitted records beyond the new leader's log and catches up.
  5. Clients redirect
    Producers and consumers refresh metadata and continue with the new leader, usually within seconds.
Pitfall
Unclean leader election trades data for availability
If every ISR member is dead, the partition is offline. Setting unclean.leader.election.enable=true lets an out-of-sync replica become leader, bringing the partition back at the cost of silently losing every committed record that replica lacked. It has been off by default since Kafka 0.11. Leave it off for business data; turning it on can make sense for metrics where availability matters more than completeness.
Tip
Durability comes from replication, not fsync
Kafka acknowledges writes once they are in the page cache of the ISR brokers; it does not fsync each message by default. The bet is that three brokers in three zones will not all lose power before the OS flushes. The standard durable configuration is replication factor 3, min.insync.replicas=2, acks=all, and unclean election disabled.
In the interview
Explaining Kafka durability
'Each partition has three replicas in three zones. Producers use acks=all with min.insync.replicas=2, so an acknowledged write is on at least two brokers and survives any single failure. If two replicas are down, writes to that partition fail rather than risk loss. Consumers read only up to the high watermark, so they never see a record that a failover could remove.'
