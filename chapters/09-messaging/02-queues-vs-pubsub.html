Part 4 / Ch 9 · Message Queues, Streams and Event-Driven Design
Lesson 2 of 7 · 10 min read

Point-to-point vs pub/sub, and choosing a broker

Two consumption models cover most uses. In a point-to-point queue, each message is delivered to one of the consumers reading the queue; adding consumers adds parallel workers that share the load (the competing consumers pattern). In publish/subscribe, each message goes to every subscriber; adding a subscriber adds a new, independent recipient. Real systems combine them: an event is published to a topic, each subscribing service gets its own copy, and within a service several instances compete for that copy.

Distinguish commands from events. A command ('ChargeCard', 'SendEmail') asks one specific handler to do something and may be rejected; it naturally goes on a point-to-point queue owned by that handler. An event ('OrderPlaced', 'PaymentFailed') states a fact that already happened, in past tense; the producer does not care who listens, so it goes on a pub/sub topic. Naming messages this way keeps ownership clear.

Two kinds of broker: queues and logs

Message queue (RabbitMQ, SQS, ActiveMQ)

Strengths
  • Per-message acknowledgment: a message is removed once processed
  • Flexible routing, priorities, per-message TTL and delays
  • Scaling consumers is trivial: add workers to the queue
Weaknesses
  • No replay: once acknowledged, a message is gone
  • Ordering weakens as soon as several consumers compete
  • Each new subscriber needs its own queue and a copy of every message
Use whenTask distribution and job queues: send this email, resize this image, call this webhook.

Log-based stream (Kafka, Kinesis, Pulsar)

Strengths
  • Messages are retained for days or forever, so consumers can replay
  • Many consumer groups read the same data independently at no extra write cost
  • Strict ordering per partition at very high throughput
Weaknesses
  • Parallelism is capped by the partition count
  • A slow or failing message blocks its whole partition
  • Per-message routing, priorities and delays are not built in
Use whenEvent streams many services consume, high-volume ingestion, CDC, rebuilding state by replay.

Tip
A quick decision rule
Need replay, several independent consumers of the same stream, or more than ~100k msg/s with ordering? Kafka (or Kinesis on AWS). Need a job queue with per-message retry, delays and minimal operations on AWS? SQS, with SNS in front for fan-out. Need complex routing (by region, priority, message type) or per-message priorities in your own infrastructure? RabbitMQ.
In production
Kafka at LinkedIn
Kafka was built at LinkedIn to move activity events and logs between systems and was open-sourced in 2011. By 2019 LinkedIn reported running Kafka at more than 7 trillion messages per day.
In the interview
Say which model you are using
Interviewers notice when 'queue' and 'topic' are used interchangeably. Be explicit: 'OrderPlaced is an event on a Kafka topic; billing, email and analytics each have their own consumer group. ResizeImage is a command on an SQS queue with ten competing workers.'
