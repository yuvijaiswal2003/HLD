Remember for the interview

  1. 01Scale up first when it's simple; scale out when you need more than one machine's capacity or need to survive a machine failure.
  2. 02Keep the app tier stateless: sessions in Redis or a signed token, files in object storage. Stateless tiers scale by adding boxes.
  3. 03L4 balancers see IPs and ports and decide per connection; L7 balancers parse HTTP and decide per request, enabling path routing, retries and gRPC balancing.
  4. 04Terminate TLS at the L7 balancer by default; re-encrypt (or use mTLS) when the internal network must be encrypted too; passthrough loses L7 routing.
  5. 05Round robin for uniform requests, least connections for variable or long-lived ones, power of two choices with many LB nodes, consistent hashing only when you need affinity.
  6. 06Use readiness checks with hysteresis for routing, never let a shared dependency fail every instance's health check, and drain connections on deploy and scale-in.
  7. 07Avoid sticky sessions: they cause uneven load, lost sessions on failure, and fight autoscaling.
  8. 08Remove the LB as a SPOF in layers: multi-AZ managed LBs or VRRP pairs inside a site, GSLB or anycast across regions.
  9. 09Autoscaling takes minutes: scale out fast, scale in slowly, and use warm pools or scheduled scaling for spikes you can predict.
  10. 10When the fleet grows, check what breaks next: DB connections, downstream rate limits, LB capacity, account quotas.
