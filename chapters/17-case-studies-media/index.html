Remember for the interview

  1. 01Video streaming splits a control plane (metadata, auth, signed manifest URLs) from a data plane (segments from the CDN). App servers never stream video bytes, and egress bandwidth is the design.
  2. 02Transcoding is an asynchronous DAG: split at keyframes, encode chunk-and-rendition tasks in parallel, stitch and package. Tasks are idempotent, and low renditions are published first.
  3. 03Adaptive bitrate is a client decision. Keyframe-aligned segments plus HLS/DASH manifests let the player switch quality at any segment boundary, and CMAF lets one set of segments serve both formats.
  4. 04Pre-position a predictable catalog on ISP-embedded caches during off-peak fill windows (Netflix Open Connect); use pull-through tiered caching with an origin shield for the long tail.
  5. 05Ride-hailing has two data paths: a firehose of disposable locations held in memory and logged to Kafka, and a trickle of trips that must be correct, held in a transactional store with a state machine.
  6. 06Index moving drivers with cells (H3 or geohash) for O(1) updates, search ring by ring, rank by road ETA, and guarantee one driver per trip with a compare-and-set reservation. Shard by city.
  7. 07File sync is content-addressed immutable blocks plus a per-namespace metadata journal: send hashes, upload only missing blocks, then commit. Metadata is never committed before its blocks are durable.
  8. 08Sync notifications are hints; clients pull changes by cursor. Detect conflicts with a compare-and-set on the parent revision and keep a conflicted copy, never last-writer-wins on device clocks.
  9. 09A seat hold is a conditional UPDATE with an expiry, the unique constraint on (show, seat) is the last guard, and payment confirmation is conditional and idempotent, with an automatic refund if the hold was lost.
  10. 10A flash sale is solved at the edge: a virtual waiting room meters admission to what inventory needs, so the booking database sees hundreds of writes per second instead of a million requests.
