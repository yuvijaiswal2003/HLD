Design Instagram Stories (24h expiring media)

Design Instagram Stories: users post photos and short videos that their followers can view for 24 hours, after which the story disappears. Viewers see a tray of rings for the accounts they follow, with unseen stories highlighted, and tap through stories that play instantly. Authors can see who viewed each story. Stories can be shared with all followers or only a Close Friends list.

1Clarify first

Write down what you would ask the interviewer before designing anything, then compare.

  • How many daily viewers and posters, and how many stories does a typical poster publish per day?
  • Must expiry be exact to the second, or is it fine if a story disappears a few minutes late?
  • After 24 hours, is the story deleted for good, or kept in the author's private archive and Highlights?
  • How long should the viewer list stay available to the author?
  • How should the tray be ordered: by recency, or by closeness to the author?
  • Are replies, reactions and stickers such as polls in scope?

2Your answer

3Hints 0/5

Stuck? Each hint gives a little more away than the last.

H1Separate logical expiry (when viewers stop seeing a story) from physical deletion (when bytes are removed). Can a read-time filter make the first exact while a lazy TTL handles the second?
H2The tray is a feed problem from Chapter 16. Push the fact that an author has an active story to followers, and pull for celebrities.
H3Check the fan-out arithmetic: 300M stories a day times an average of 200 followers is far too many writes if every story triggers fan-out. What is the minimum event that changes a viewer's tray?
H4The seen state can be a single timestamp per viewer and author pair rather than a set of story IDs.
H5Instant playback is mostly prefetching on the client: the next story and the next author's first story.

4Model solution

Try your own answer first. The solution follows seven steps: requirements, estimates, API, data model, architecture, deep dives, trade-offs.

1. Requirements

  • Functional: post photo or video stories to followers or Close Friends; the tray shows followed accounts with active stories, unseen first; tap-through playback; the author sees a viewer list; stories vanish for viewers 24 h after posting; the author can keep them in a private archive and add them to Highlights.
  • Non-functional: a story is visible to followers within a few seconds; playback starts with no visible delay; expiry is exact for viewers; the tray loads in under ~200 ms at p99; storage cost reflects the fact that most media dies in a day.

2. Estimates

text
Viewers: 500M per day; posters: 25% = 125M, 2.4 stories each ~ 300M stories/day
  300M / 86,400                     ~ 3.5k new stories/s, ~10k/s at peak
Media: 60% photos x 300 KB + 40% 15 s videos x 3.75 MB (2 Mbps) ~ 1.7 MB average
  x2 for renditions                 ~ 3.4 MB stored per story
  300M x 3.4 MB                     ~ 1 PB/day; the live 24 h window is ~1 PB
Tray loads: 500M x 12 app opens = 6B/day ~ 70k/s, ~200k/s at peak
Story plays: 500M x 40 stories = 20B/day ~ 230k/s, ~600k/s at peak (CDN)
  20B x 1.7 MB = 34 PB/day / 86,400  ~ 390 GB/s ~ 3.1 Tbps of egress
Seen events: 20B/day                ~ 230k writes/s; x 30 B ~ 600 GB/day of viewer rows
Fan-out if every story were pushed: 300M x 200 followers = 60B writes/day ~ 700k/s

Three numbers drive the design. Egress of ~3 Tbps means a CDN with aggressive prefetch. Seen-event writes of ~230k/s need a wide-column store with TTLs. And the naive fan-out of ~700k tray writes/s is the thing to cut down in the deep dive.

5. High-level design

The author's app compresses and uploads media while they are still adding stickers, then creates the story. The story service writes a row with expires_at and a TTL, and emits an event. Fan-out workers add the author to each follower's tray, or only to Close Friends for a restricted story. Opening the app reads the viewer's tray sorted set, merges in celebrity authors pulled at read time, marks rings unseen by comparing each author's latest timestamp with the viewer's seen timestamp, and ranks them. Tapping a ring fetches that author's active stories, with signed media URLs that expire at the story's expiry, and the app prefetches ahead. Seen events are batched to the seen service, which updates the viewer's seen state and appends to the story's viewer list.

6. Deep dives

Expiry done right. Don't rely on a cron job deleting rows at exactly 24 h; a backlog would leave stories visible late. Use two layers. Logical expiry is a read-time filter, expires_at > now(), on every query and tray entry, which makes expiry exact to the second however far behind cleanup is. Physical deletion is lazy and cheap: a Cassandra row TTL, an object-storage lifecycle rule on the live prefix, and Redis key TTLs. Cassandra TTLs leave tombstones, so use time-window compaction, which groups rows by write time into SSTables and drops a whole SSTable once everything in it has expired, instead of compacting tombstones row by row. Media URLs are signed to expire at expires_at, and the CDN's max-age is capped at the remaining lifetime, so a cached copy stops being reachable on time. Archiving copies the story row to the archive table and the media to the archive prefix at creation (a server-side copy), which keeps the live tier purely disposable.

The tray without a fan-out storm. A viewer's tray changes membership only when an author goes from no active stories to some, or back. A third story posted the same afternoon only changes that author's latest timestamp. So fan out only on the first story in an author's active window, adding the author to followers' trays with the expiry of that window. Subsequent stories update one latest_ts in a per-author cache, which the tray service fetches in a single multi-get for the ~50–100 authors in a tray. That cuts fan-out from about 300M × 200 to about 125M × 200 = 25B writes a day, roughly 290k/s spread across a Redis cluster, and authors with millions of followers are never fanned out at all; they are pulled and merged at read time, the hybrid model from Chapter 16. The seen state is one timestamp per (viewer, author) pair, and a ring is unseen if the author's latest_ts is greater than the viewer's seen timestamp, which is far cheaper than storing a seen bit per story.

Playback with no visible delay. The client does most of the work. On tray load it prefetches the first story of the first few rings. While story N plays, it prefetches N+1 and the first story of the next author. Videos are encoded short and small enough that the first bytes arrive in one round trip from the edge. Viewer lists are written at ~230k/s into Cassandra partitions keyed by story_id, which are wide but bounded (a story with 10M views is a large partition, so a celebrity's viewer list is split into bucketed partitions). The view count is a separate counter, so the author sees a number instantly and pages through names on demand.

5Follow-up questions

  • A user reports that a deleted story was still visible to a follower for a minute. Where could it have been served from, and how do you close the gap?
  • How would you rank the tray by closeness instead of recency, and where does that computation run?
  • Add replies and emoji reactions that go to the author's inbox. What changes?
  • How would you support a user switching a story's audience from Close Friends to all followers after posting?
  • Legal requires that expired media be unrecoverable within 24 hours of expiry. What changes in the storage design?
